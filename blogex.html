About

Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam, popularly known as A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, was an Indian aerospace scientist and the 11th President of India, serving from 2002 to 2007. He was born on October 15, 1931, in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Coming from a modest background, Kalam worked diligently throughout his life, rising to become one of India's most respected scientists and leaders. He played a pivotal role in India's civilian space program and military missile development, earning him the nickname “Missile Man of India.”Dr. Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam, popularly known as A.P.J. Achievements

Dr. Kalam's contributions to science and technology have had a lasting impact. He led India's efforts in nuclear weapons tests in 1998 and was key to the development of the Agni and Prithvi missiles. As a scientist at ISRO and DRDO, he played vital roles in India's space and defense advancements. In recognition of his work, he received India's highest civilian award, the Bharat Ratna, in 1997, along with the Padma Bhushan (1981) and Padma Vibhushan (1990). He also authored several inspirational books such as “Wings of Fire,” “Ignited Minds,” and “India 2020.”Dr. Kalam's contributions to science and technology have had a lasting impact. Education Life

Dr. Kalam's educational journey began in Rameswaram, where he attended Schwartz Higher Secondary School. He went on to study physics at St. Joseph’s College, Tiruchirappalli, graduating in 1954. Later, he pursued aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology (MIT). Despite facing financial and academic challenges, Kalam excelled through sheer determination and hard work. His time at MIT was especially transformative, solidifying his passion for flight and rocket technology.Dr. Kalam's educational journey began in Rameswaram, where he attended Schwartz Higher Secondary School. Marriage

He often said that he considered the youth of India his family.
